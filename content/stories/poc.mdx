<Step media="stories/poc/IMG_7041">The air smelled like cedar as the train curved along the coast. The morning mist clung to the mountains like a shroud, and I could hear the distant sound of temple bells carried on the wind. Through the window, I watched small villages pass by—clusters of wooden houses with steep roofs designed to shed the heavy snow that would come in winter. The train slowed as we approached a tunnel, and for a moment, everything went dark except for the soft glow of the overhead lights. When we emerged, the landscape had changed completely. Farmers worked in the fields, their backs bent under the weight of tradition and necessity. The train continued its journey, carrying us deeper into the heart of Tohoku, where the old ways still held sway and the pace of life moved to the rhythm of seasons rather than schedules.</Step>

<Step media="stories/poc/IMG_7045">Morning light hit the water—thin silver across the bay. The fishing boats were already out, their nets cast wide in hopes of a good catch. I stood on the observation deck, watching the sun rise over the mountains, painting the sky in shades of pink and gold. The air was crisp and clean, carrying the salt tang of the sea and the earthy scent of the forest behind me. Seagulls wheeled overhead, their cries echoing across the water. A small group of elderly women walked along the shore, collecting shells and seaweed for their evening meal. Their movements were slow and deliberate, each step a testament to lives lived close to the land and sea. The bay stretched out before me, its surface rippling gently in the morning breeze. In the distance, I could see the outline of other islands, their peaks shrouded in mist. This was a place where time seemed to move differently, where the ancient rhythms of nature still held sway over the modern world.</Step>
